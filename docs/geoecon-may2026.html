Central Thesis

The Iran war is not merely an energy shock — it is a multilayered disruption of the global economy in which resilience is replacing efficiency as the organizing principle. Geography and control over it has been fundamentally repriced, with strategic choke points again shaping global economic outcomes. Three themes defined April 2026: global systems are being reshaped; energy shocks are spilling into food insecurity; and insurance markets are driving restructuring. (source: GeoEcon-May2026.pdf)

Three Organizing Themes

1. Global systems are being reshaped. The Iran war exposed the vulnerability of fuel logistics and power-dependent digital infrastructure, accelerating decentralized energy, cybersecurity integration and resilience-focused operations. Supply chain disruptions highlight the limits of global efficiency models. (source: GeoEcon-May2026.pdf)

2. Energy shocks spill into food insecurity. Higher energy prices feed into fertilizer markets, raising production costs and threatening agricultural output. Higher food prices increase the likelihood of political and economic instability, most acutely in import-dependent regions. (source: GeoEcon-May2026.pdf)

3. Insurance markets drive restructuring. Rising war-risk premiums and selective coverage directly affect global trade flows. As private insurers reduce exposure, governments are stepping in with sovereign-backed mechanisms, contributing to the emergence of regionalized insurance systems. The ability to insure trade is becoming a strategic tool. (source: GeoEcon-May2026.pdf)
